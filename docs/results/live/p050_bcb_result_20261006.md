## 4. Flight 2 low-battery descent: measured against recollection

Operator recollection (2 October): "the battery got too low and the drone descended" after all episodes were recorded.

Measured, all three flights alike:

- The pilot switches GUIDED → LOITER, lowers the throttle stick (1540–1606 µs to about 1255–1325 µs), the aircraft descends at 0.7–0.9 m/s (min climb rate −87, −79, −80 cm/s) for 7–10 s, then the stick goes to 988 µs at about 1.25 m, 2.3 m and 1.6 m respectively, and the aircraft lands and disarms.
- Flight 2's final descent matches Flights 1 and 3 in rate, shape and duration. No FCU failsafe, error record, LAND or RTL mode in any of them.
- Flight 2 voltage at the start of the descent was 14.76 V (lowest of the three, 14.72 V minimum), with no collapse. Cell count and the operator's low-battery limit are not in the evidence, so whether 14.7 V is "too low" cannot be judged here.

Conclusion: the logs show a **stick-commanded** descent identical to the other flights' landings, not an FCU battery failsafe or an uncommanded drop. They cannot show the pilot's reason, so the low-battery cause rests on the operator's statement and is **unverified**.

One discrepancy: the descent started about 10 s before the recorded Flight 2 `O3` end marker (GUIDED exit 14:55:05.2, O3 end 14:55:15.3), so the statement that all episodes were already recorded is not borne out by the markers. O3 had 24.0 s of its 34.1 s inside GUIDED.

Command-level yaw is saturated four times more often in Flight 2, but the different scenes and trajectories make that descriptive only. Forward/lateral/vertical commands never exceed 0.06 m/s, so following in all three flights is effectively yaw-only.

**Stale/invalid samples.** Six samples across the baselines carry a non-zero command while `target_fresh` was false and the reason was `trusted_locked_normal`. This fits the already recorded freshness/callback-ordering uncertainty in the diagnostic pairing, but it is not resolved here, and under the safety rule it is reported, not dismissed.

**FCU response to recovery (Flight 2).** During activation 1 (0.86 s) the FCU yaw rate reached about 6°/s (0.10 rad/s = 5.7°/s) for roughly 0.6 s. The aircraft was already yawing at similar rates before and after, and alignment is ±0.5 s, so the response is **consistent with the command but not isolated**. Activation 2 (0.16 s) is indistinguishable from background yaw. **Command bound versus aircraft response.** The controller limit is 0.20 rad/s = 11.5°/s. The FCU log shows larger yaw rates in every GUIDED phase: desired rate maximum 17.5 / 27.4 / 20.7°/s and achieved rate maximum 15.7 / 14.5 / 14.9°/s (Flights 1 / 2 / 3); Flight 2 desired-rate p95 is 11.5°/s. The cause is not established (the FCU desired rate is not the controller command and no pilot input was present). Command-level bounds therefore do not bound the measured aircraft response, in baselines as well as the candidate.

A third recovery activation (0.43 s) occurs after GUIDED exit and is excluded.

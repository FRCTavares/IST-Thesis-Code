### 2.1 Video-to-controller alignment (gyro method, added 6 October)

The field MCAP has no image topic and the MKV carries no camera source stamp. The offset between video packet time and the aircraft clock was therefore estimated **without any tracker output**: for each pair of consecutive video frames the global horizontal image shift (sparse optical flow, RANSAC similarity) was compared with the gyro yaw angle integrated over the same interval from `/mavros/imu/data_raw` (49 Hz, bag header stamps), scanning the offset `delta` in `source_launch_rel = video_pts + delta` (the convention of the earlier evaluations).

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Frame pairs used | 750 | 680 | 820 |
| Best `delta` (s) | **0.60** | **0.22** | **1.02** |
| Correlation at best `delta` | 0.967 | 0.980 | 0.997 |
| `delta` range within 1 % of peak (s) | 0.53–0.67 | 0.16–0.29 | 0.95–1.10 |
| `delta` by time window | 0.57–0.62 | 0.22–0.24 | 1.02–1.03 |
| Implied scale (px/rad) | 407 | 430 | 447 |
| Previous tracker-geometry fit (s) | 0.74 | 0.32 | 1.12 |

Reading: the offset is constant within each flight (no drift across windows), the scale is consistent across flights and plausible for a 640 px camera, and the independent method lands 0.10–0.14 s below the tracker-geometry fit in every flight (a consistent shift; tracker output latency is a plausible, untested explanation). Alignment uncertainty is taken as about ±0.1 s from the peak width, plus about 0.1–0.2 s from frame sampling (about 4–5 frames/s); this replaces the ±0.5 s sensitivity bound used before. Remaining assumptions: IMU header stamps share the controller's ROS clock to within tens of milliseconds, and the offset is the same for all frames (supported by the window results). ## 4. Flight 2 low-battery descent: measured against recollection

Operator recollection (2 October): "the battery got too low and the drone descended" after all episodes were recorded.

Measured, all three flights alike:

- The pilot switches GUIDED → LOITER, lowers the throttle stick (1540–1606 µs to about 1255–1325 µs), the aircraft descends at 0.7–0.9 m/s (min climb rate −87, −79, −80 cm/s) for 7–10 s, then the stick goes to 988 µs at about 1.25 m, 2.3 m and 1.6 m respectively, and the aircraft lands and disarms.
- Flight 2's final descent matches Flights 1 and 3 in rate, shape and duration. No FCU failsafe, error record, LAND or RTL mode in any of them.
- Flight 2 voltage at the start of the descent was 14.76 V (lowest of the three, 14.72 V minimum), with no collapse. Cell count and the operator's low-battery limit are not in the evidence, so whether 14.7 V is "too low" cannot be judged here.

Conclusion: the logs show a **stick-commanded** descent identical to the other flights' landings, not an FCU battery failsafe or an uncommanded drop. They cannot show the pilot's reason, so the low-battery cause rests on the operator's statement and is **unverified**.

One discrepancy: the descent started about 10 s before the recorded Flight 2 `O3` end marker (GUIDED exit 14:55:05.2, O3 end 14:55:15.3), so the statement that all episodes were already recorded is not borne out by the markers. O3 had 24.0 s of its 34.1 s inside GUIDED.

### 6.3 Frozen metric versus post hoc metric

- **Frozen:** correct-person reacquisition within 10.0 s of the loss, censored at 10.0 s. Baseline A: the person was absent for 15.2 s, so the horizon ends before the person is back (uninformative, censored). Candidate: the person returned at 9.6 s but trusted control resumed only 14.6 s after the loss (censored). **Both censored; they cannot be separated.**
- **Post hoc, labelled exploratory (not predeclared):** controller return minus physical return: +0.73 s versus +4.89 s. The candidate was slower to regain trusted control after the person came back.
- **Confounds, not separable here:** absence length differed (15.2 s versus 9.6 s); the return position, the route and the lighting differ; the tracker re-acquired under a new ID in the candidate flight; the 0.86 s yaw recovery at loss start cannot be linked to a delay 10 s later. This is one pair of repeated observations in different scenes, not independent replicates and not a statistical comparison.

### 6.4 Eligibility and the 3 s precondition

The frozen precondition is at least 3.0 s of visible trusted control before the loss. An unbroken-run measure fails (nf_before under 1 s) only because 330 of 337 gaps in Flight 1 (and 256 of 257, 319 of 323 in Flights 2 and 3) are sub-0.5 s flicker that resets the run. A flicker-tolerant measure is more faithful: trusted time in the preceding 5 s was 4.04 s (Flight 1 O2), 4.12 s (Flight 2 O2) and 2.49 s (Flight 3 O1; person visible, not a loss). The first two satisfy the precondition. This is a post hoc definition and is reported as such; the strict figures are retained in `bcb_timeline.json`.

### Wrong-person exposure (geometric, gyro-aligned)

Method: the earlier overlap criterion was re-implemented (annotated person box versus the recorded `/target_memory_mars` box; IoU at least 0.30 and strictly larger than any annotated competitor; nearest message within 500 ms; frames of the selected GUIDED window with the target annotated). **It reproduces the published counts exactly at the old offsets** (Flight 3 at 1.12 s: target 446, distractor 33, no output 70, unresolved 5; Flights 1 and 2 target-overlap 592 and 543). It was then evaluated at the gyro-derived offsets, and distractor frames were merged into intervals (each frame covering half the gap to its neighbours) and intersected with the controller command series.

| | Offsets tested (s) | Distractor-overlap frames | Distractor intervals (s) | Non-zero command inside (s) | Unresolved-geometry frames (not excluded) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Flight 1, Baseline A | 0.53–0.74 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 27–33 of 625 |
| Flight 2, Candidate | 0.16–0.32 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 6–9 of 575 |
| Flight 3, Baseline B | 0.94–1.10 | 33–35 of 554 | 10.4–10.7 | **9.3–9.6** | 5–14 of 554 |

Flight 3 intervals (gyro offset 1.02 s, UTC): 15:03:06.06–06.84 (0.78 s, 0.73 s non-zero, mean yaw command 0.14 rad/s, up to the 0.20 limit, immediately before the 12.2 s tracker loss in O1); 15:04:38.98–39.18; 15:04:41.64–42.32; and **15:04:42.70–51.74 (9.04 s, 8.20 s non-zero) during the O3 distractor crossing, with a mean yaw command of 0.026 rad/s (maximum 0.088 rad/s) and at most 0.033 m/s forward**. The TIM output therefore followed a distractor for about 9 s during O3, but the commands in that stretch were small (the distractor was near the image centre); the first burst was brief but at the yaw limit. Tracker IDs involved: 42 and 73 (earlier evaluation).

Qualifications: geometric overlap with an annotated person box is not a proof of identity; frames are sparse (about 4.4 per second), so interval ends carry 0.1–0.2 s; the three mixed target/distractor frames are counted in the distractor class by the strict-win rule; the aircraft's physical response to these specific commands is not isolated; the 27–33 and 6–9 unresolved frames in Flights 1 and 2 mean "zero distractor frames" is a measured lower bound on exposure, not proof of zero.

**Outcome after the episodes (self-correction).** After the 9.0 s stretch (tracker ID 73, frames 1364–1395) the TIM output returned to the annotated target (track ID 53) within 0.32 s (frame 1397 at the 1.02 s offset) and overlapped the target in 91.9 % of the 74 remaining frames of the window. After the 0.78 s burst (ID 42, frames 1001–1003) the output went to no-output, so the controller held zero command, and overlapped the target again 12.4 s later (frame 1054). The authority therefore corrected itself in both cases without operator action. This lowers the severity of the episodes but does not remove the exposure: the wrong-person command time above still counts.

**Under the #50 safety rule, wrong-person non-zero command is safety-blocking. The baseline configuration itself (not only the candidate) produced a measurable wrong-person episode in Flight 3.** This is a failure case of the TIM-MARS authority under a distractor crossing, and it must stay in the results and limitations.

Image-to-command timing is now estimated (section 2.1), no longer unresolved; callback-order and command-to-actuation timing remain unresolved.
